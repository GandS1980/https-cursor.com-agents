# Video editor

Turn a raw recording into a finished short-form video, and get better with every correction the user gives.

## First time: set everything up

When the user first runs this skill:

1. **Download the tools.** Install these on their computer:
   - **ffmpeg**: cuts and mixes the video.
   - **HyperFrames**: captions and graphics.
   - **yt-dlp**: saves reels, so you can study creators whose style they like.
   - **Whisper**: transcribes the recording with word timings.

   Use the official downloads and avoid anything that needs their password if you can. Tell them what you're installing and why.
2. **Ask where they want their project folder**, then create it. Suggest Documents or Desktop on a Mac, so it's backed up by iCloud (Documents with OneDrive on Windows). Call it "video editing", with four folders inside:
   - **raw footage**: their recordings.
   - **examples**: their best-performing videos, or videos from creators whose style they want to copy.
   - **assets**: things like b-roll and event footage.
   - **output**: everything you make for them.
3. **Get to know their style.** Ask:
   - where their scripts live;
   - whether they read them word for word or use them as talking points;
   - what kind of videos they make;
   - whether they want you to handle colour.

   Then study the videos in examples. Remember what you learn, and use it on every edit.

## Editing a video

- **Get the script.** If they use it as talking points, listen to the recording and use what they actually said.
- **Choose the best take.** People repeat lines, trip and restart. Keep the cleanest take of each line, and never keep a line twice.
- **Cut out the dead space** between sentences, the restarts, the "um"s and the asides.
- **Cut only between sentences.** Leave the natural pauses and breaths inside a sentence alone; trimming those makes the video feel choppy.
- **Join seamlessly.** Leave only a short beat between sentences, about 0.15 seconds. Long pauses are the first thing people notice.
- **Never clip a word.** Words ending in t, k, s or th, and quiet first words like "if" or "so", get cut off easily. Before you render, listen to the first and last word of every piece. When the user asks you to cut a phrase, check that the word before it keeps its last sound and the word after it keeps its first.
- **Captions.** Short chunks of a few words, spelled like the script, with names spelled correctly.
- **Title.** A short hook title at the top while the hook line is being said for the first 5 seconds.
- **Keep everything away from the edges of the frame.** TikTok and Reels zoom in and crop the sides.

## Visuals

**Talking heads and video essays** are edited the same way. When they mention something, show it at that moment:

- a headline that says what they're saying;
- the real number or chart;
- a company's logo, then what it makes;
- a photo of the thing they named.

Find these yourself: open the article and screenshot it at full quality. Only add a visual if it adds information; a logo just because a name was said doesn't. Keep each one up until that point ends, but no longer than 5 seconds.

**Vlogs** are carried by their own footage. Look through every clip in assets first. Play each clip full screen, with their voice over it, when they talk about what's in it.

**Screen recordings:** zoom in on what matters, then stay there. Keep effects few. Cover anything private on their screen.

## Colour

Don't add filters unless they ask; most people do colour in their own editor. If they want you to, ask for a screenshot of their usual settings and get as close as you can. Tell them it won't be exactly the same, because every editor's sliders work differently.

## Versions and feedback

- Name every render v1, v2, v3, and keep the old ones.
- Before you hand a version over, check the sound is in sync and look at frames from it.
- Tell them what changed.
- They'll give notes with timestamps. Change only what they named, then make the next version.

## Keep learning

Every note they give is a rule for next time. Save their corrections in the skill and read them again before every edit. When they finish a video in their own editor, ask for it, compare it with your version and learn from what they changed.
